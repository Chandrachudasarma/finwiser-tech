Broken-link connector — design exploration

One small element: a severed chain/relay link that sits in the 86×560 slot between two specialist columns, anchored on the column mid-line, with the whole row scaled to 0.84 in the real slide. It must read broken connection from across a room and feel deliberate, not glitchy. Each treatment is shown three ways: true size in an 86×220 slot, enlarged 3× to judge craft, and 3×·0.84 to confirm it survives the shrink.

Recommendation

Go with Treatment 1 — diagonal oblong links, snapped in the middle with a torn edge and a "snap!" burst.

Two identical capsule links sit on the natural diagonal that interlocked links fall along, then snap apart in the middle: the two inner ends are torn with a few sharp zigzag teeth, the halves are pulled apart with a clear gap, and a small starburst of short radiating dashes marks the violent instant of breaking. It reads as a literal broken chain at a glance, it is unmistakably deliberate (icon-grade flat shape, not a glitch), and it carries the most "force" of any option — the burst sells the snap from across a room.

Because it lives at true size in an 86-wide slot scaled to 0.84, the teeth and dashes mush if drawn too fine, so there are two builds: a simplified true-size version (bold strokes, 3 chunky teeth, 4 radiating dashes, an open gap) that still reads "snapped" when small, and a detailed enlarged version for craft. The first specimen row below proves the simplified build survives the shrink.

Alternates kept for comparison: T2 cracked ring (cleanest single-link silhouette), T4 tension (most dynamic), T3 shattered and T5 severed cable (more decorative, soften when shrunk).
